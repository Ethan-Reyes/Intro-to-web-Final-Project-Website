Philosophy & Christianity

How I think about faith, knowledge, and why belief can be reasonable.

Where I'm Coming From

I came to the Christian faith in 2023 and, after a period in Non-Denonminational Protestanism and then Eastern Orthodoxy, entered full communion with the Catholic Church as a Byzatine Catholic.

St. Augustine shaped how I think about grace, the mind, and love. St. Athanasius shaped how I understand the Incarnation and God stooping down to meet us. I pair them with classical logic and metaphysics: Leibniz, Aquinas, and Bonaventure.

Faith and Knowledge

Science itself runs on trust. It assumes physical laws stay uniform, that our senses are reliable, and that logic and math are valid, and none of that can be proven from inside a lab.

Insisting that only lab-verifiable claims count is self-defeating, because that rule can't be verified in a lab either. The biblical word pistis means rational trust and allegiance, not a leap in the dark.

The Third Path Framework

This is my own attempt at a problem in Leibniz's Principle of Sufficient Reason: every contingent thing needs a sufficient reason, which points to God as the first cause.

The problem

If God's reason to create was mandatory, creation becomes necessary and free will disappears (modal collapse). If God created on a whim, that's a brute fact with no reason behind it, which breaks the principle.

The third path

Ground creation in God's essence, which is love. Love requires real, uncoerced freedom, so God voluntarily limits His control (kenosis) to leave room for creatures with genuine agency. The cause stays rooted in God's nature, and our contingency lives inside the space He made for it.

Defending the Resurrection

The sudden conversions of a persecutor like St. Paul and a skeptic like St. James need an adequate cause.

People die for ideas they were taught, but the first witnesses were in a position to know if it was a hoax. They accepted torture and death anyway. Group hallucinations across different places and settings also don't fit what psychology tells us.

Science, Miracles, and Morality

Physics accepts strange things like quantum entanglement and dark matter based on their effects. Accepting those while rejecting historical miracles outright looks like a double standard driven by naturalistic assumptions.

Appealing to a multiverse to explain away fine-tuning swaps one metaphysical idea for another. And if ethics is only evolutionary survival preference, objective right and wrong disappear without God as their ground.
