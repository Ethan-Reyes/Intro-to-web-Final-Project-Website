Experiences & Influences

The games, people, and habits that made me who I am, and who I'm choosing to be.

The Big Picture

The biggest thing I've learned so far is that I used to define myself by what other people thought of me, and now I try to define myself by my own values and choices. I can't make anyone return my loyalty, affection, or effort. I can control how I act, what I stand for, what boundaries I keep, and who I decide to become.

I also care a lot. I get really invested, I work hard, and I stay loyal. That's helped me in school, work, wrestling, gaming, and my creative projects, but it can get unhealthy if I don't keep it in check. So I'm not trying to care less, I'm trying to aim it in the right direction.

Video Games

Games have been there for me my whole life, even when friendships were rough. I started with easier stuff and slowly got pulled toward harder, story-heavy games. The challenge and the stories changed how I think about failure, perseverance, and who I want to be. The full breakdown is on the Games page.

What it gave me: creativity, challenge, and a way to express myself.

My Parents

My parents have backed me up through mistakes and hard stretches, and they made sacrifices for me and my siblings. A lot of the stability, work ethic, and character I have comes from the foundation they gave me. I'm really grateful for that.

What it gave me: a foundation to grow from.

My Twin Brother

Growing up next to someone my exact age made me competitive and pushed me to hold myself to a high standard. It also got complicated, and that taught me I need an identity that isn't just "the twin."

I'm still working on defining myself by my own values and goals instead of measuring myself against him. He's a big influence, and honestly not an easy one to sum up.

What it gave me: competitiveness, and a push toward independence.

Wrestling

Wrestling was one of the hardest things I've ever done, physically and mentally, and I loved it. I made real connections through it and learned discipline, self-control, leadership, and courage.

The big lesson: being tired and uncomfortable doesn't automatically mean I should quit. Sometimes perseverance just means keeping going.

What it gave me: discipline and perseverance.

High School

High school changed how I see friendship. I learned that friendships can be taken for granted, that people change, and that I can't build my identity on comparing myself to everyone around me.

I also learned that becoming a better person doesn't happen all at once. It's something I keep working on.

What it gave me: a clearer view of friendship and identity.

A Meaningful Relationship

One close relationship in my junior year of high school ended up being one of the most meaningful stretches of my life. It was short compared to everything else, but it changed how I think about love, friendship, vulnerability, loss, and forgiveness. I still count that person and that time as part of my story, and will always treasure my times with A.

What it gave me: lessons about love, loss, and boundaries.

Faith

I spent a lot of my earlier life outside of Christianity before coming to it. Now it's my framework for thinking about forgiveness, accountability, compassion, service, and how people change.

It also matches how I handle hard questions in general: dig into the ideas, compare the explanations, and see how everything fits together. The Philosophy page goes deeper.

What it gave me: a moral and spiritual framework.

Engineering and Problem Solving

I'm going into engineering and automation because I like solving problems, both the practical kind and the abstract kind. I break things down, figure out the constraints, try a fix, test it, and learn from what fails.

That same habit shows up in my hobbies, my creative projects, and even how I think through personal questions. More on the Career page.

What it gave me: a direction for my problem-solving.
