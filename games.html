Games

Games are more than a hobby for me. They're how I relax, how I challenge myself, and how I create.

Where I Started

I started out on simpler games like Minecraft, Terraria, and Call of Duty. They were a big part of growing up, and they were there for me even when friendships were hard or people came and went.

Where I Ended Up

As I got older I got pulled toward harder, deeper, story-heavy games. These days that means Dark Souls, Dark Souls III, Bloodborne, Elden Ring, Lords of the Fallen, and Counter-Strike.

I don't see them as just entertainment. The stories, atmosphere, characters, and difficulty changed how I think about perseverance, identity, morality, failure, and growing as a person.

Platinum Trophies

I've gotten the platinum in four of them. It took a lot of failing to get there, and that's honestly the point.

My Own Game Idea: Wolf

Wolf is a dark-fantasy game I came up with, and it's more than just an idea to me. It's built around the questions I got interested in as I grew up: morality, obedience, conscience, guilt, redemption, humanity, faith, and what our choices end up costing.

The story

You play a Church hunter who starts out thinking he's just doing his job. Slowly he realizes there's a lot of humanity and moral complexity in the people he's been ordered to eliminate.

The idea behind it

The mechanics and the story are both meant to show the tension between a darker way of living and a more compassionate one. Wolf is a good example of how the way I've grown has started showing up in what I create.

What games gave me: creativity, a real challenge, and a way to express myself.
